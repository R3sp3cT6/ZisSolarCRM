# Spatial sampling of native-hourly GFS — frozen protocol, 12 October 2026

Goal remains monthly hourly WAPE below 5%, not merely a small net monthly energy error. The preceding local MOS trial was progress (new code, causal tests and archived evidence), but did not meet its cross-period gate and was not activated.

Hypothesis: using only the nearest NWP grid cell can magnify cloud-position errors. Bilinear sampling or a small fixed spatial neighbourhood may represent local uncertainty better. This is spatial sampling at every original hourly step; no three-hour aggregation and no hindsight time shifts.

Primary background: [Hansen et al., irradiance forecasts and spatial averaging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X15005897), [regional weather variability in NWP solar forecasting](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0038092X17300774). Smoothing can reduce RMSE while missing ramps, and results elsewhere do not prove improved Resen WAPE.

## Fixed experiment, no parameter search

1. Reuse the original archived NOAA GFS 0.25-degree GRIB fields already downloaded for delivery dates 21 September–11 October. Same previous-day 12 UTC runs, original publication before 18 UTC cutoff and SHA256. Decode the grid surrounding latitude 41.068056, longitude 21.036111. No new API, payment or downloaded historical observations.
2. Two candidates only: bilinear weights of the four enclosing grid points; equal mean of the 3×3 grid centred on the nearest point (approximately 42×56 km between outer centres at Resen). The latter deliberately represents spatial uncertainty, not site-measured weather. No fit of neighbour weights, selected radius, changed geometry, time smoothing, cloud-regime routing or adjustment after seeing scores.
3. For each original accumulated/averaged radiation field, compute each spatial sample, then de-average using the existing native-hourly energy conversion. Confirm grid scan/layout, units, cycles, bounds and every point; never substitute zero for missing/bad data. Bilinear weights sum to 1 and reproduce a linear field exactly. Nearest control must exactly reproduce the earlier native-hourly archive.
4. Same original plant response/calibration, thermal inputs and causal v3.2 hourly gain as the preceding site-learning experiment. Compare archived v3.2, native-hourly nearest control, native-hourly bilinear and native-hourly 3×3. Only spatial sampling varies between the three native-hourly candidates. v3.2 is the original lower-temporal-resolution contextual baseline, not the control isolating spatial sampling.
5. Score all common daylight/positive-actual intervals, all 21 dates, September and October separately. No target-derived outage exclusions, missing-target interpolation or best-day selection. Dates are already explored: retrospective diagnostic evidence only. Preserve WAPE, MAE, RMSE, bias, daily scores, hourly predictions, input provenance and all failures. Five-thousand paired-day bootstrap replicates, seed42, does not remove repeated-development bias.
6. Candidate chosen on September WAPE only before October evaluation. An additional live trial is justified only if the chosen variant improves WAPE at least 2% relatively versus both v3.2 and nearest control in both periods, without overall RMSE worsening. No official replacement based on this historical test. If it fails, keep existing production models and preserve the negative result; do not retune October or change the gate.

Freeze implementation and input checksums before scoring. Independently verify geometry, energy conversion, exact controls and scores. Append all decoded numeric fields and predictions to research SQL; large original binaries stay local with their existing checksums. Production code, user data and plant control are untouched by this experiment.
